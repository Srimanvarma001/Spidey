Spiders are air-breathing arthropods with eight legs, chelicerae that carry fangs, and spinnerets that extrude silk. They form the largest order of arachnids and are found on every continent except Antarctica. Unlike insects, a spider's body has two segments, the cephalothorax and the abdomen, joined by a narrow stalk called the pedicel.

A spider walks with an alternating gait: four legs stay on the ground while the other four swing forward. The legs are extended by hydraulic pressure, not by muscle, which is why a dead spider's legs curl inward. Most species build webs to catch prey, though many hunt on foot, and a few, such as jumping spiders, rely on sharp eyesight.
